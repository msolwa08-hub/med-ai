# The depth bar — every research dossier must hit this

The test: a consultant in this specialty reads the dossier and finds nothing missing, nothing
naive, and at least a few things they'd have had to stop and think about. If it reads like a
final-year student's summary, it fails. Breadth of differential AND depth of reasoning.

Each department dossier (`research/<dept>.md`) must contain:

## 1. The consultant's mental model
How a specialist actually frames a patient walking onto this ward — the questions behind the
questions, the two or three things they are always secretly ruling out, the tempo.

## 2. Presenting syndromes — the full set, each worked to depth
For EVERY common presenting complaint/syndrome in the department (aim for comprehensive, not a
top-5), give:
- **Differential — three tiers:** (a) common/bread-and-butter, (b) dangerous / must-not-miss,
  (c) the zebras & the ones consultants specifically consider that juniors forget (e.g. for a
  metabolic acidosis: RTA types 1/2/4 with their discriminators; for hyponatraemia: the full
  volume-status algorithm incl. SIADH causes, cerebral salt wasting; for a young stroke:
  vasculitis, HIV, antiphospholipid, dissection).
- **Discriminating history** — the specific questions that split the differential.
- **Discriminating examination** — the specific signs that split it, incl. the subtle/normal.
- **Investigations & their INTERPRETATION nuance** — not just "do a gas" but the urine anion
  gap, the delta-delta, the transtubular potassium gradient, the trend that matters, the
  classic trap in reading it.
- **Management anchors** — SA STG/EML first-line, when to escalate, the time-critical steps.
- **Consultant traps & pearls** — the specific errors that harm, the "always/never".

## 3. The normals
Reference values and normal findings that define this ward — what normal looks like here, so
abnormal is recognisable. Include age/physiology-adjusted where relevant.

## 4. Cross-cutting investigation interpretation (deep)
The department's signature workups end-to-end: e.g. Medicine — acid-base (full RTA workup,
osmolar gap, delta-delta), hyponatraemia algorithm, anaemia (micro/normo/macro with the
discriminators), AKI (pre/renal/post + urine indices), LFT patterns. Surgery — the acute
abdomen investigation ladder, the septic post-op screen. Give the numbers and the thresholds.

## 5. Scores, charts, and structured tools
Every scoring system/chart the discipline uses, with the inputs and interpretation bands, so
they can be built as structured components (not free text).

## 6. SA-specific reality
TB/HIV integration points, STG/EML formulary specifics, the district-hospital resource
constraints that change decisions, notifiable conditions, medico-legal touchpoints.

## Sourcing
SA sources first (STG/EML by edition, national guidelines), global second (name them). Mark any
item resting on established knowledge rather than a fetched source as [EK]. Cite per section.

## Format
Dense, scannable, specific numbers/thresholds. Long is fine — this is a reference, not a summary.
Target 600-1200 lines per department. Do not pad; every line should earn its place.
